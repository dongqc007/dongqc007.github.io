abstract: "The finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) method(A. Z. Elsherbeni and V. Demir, The finite-difference time-domain method for electromagnetics with MATLAB simulations. Raleigh, NC: SciTech Pub., 2009.) requires truncating the computational domain to a finite space using absorbing boundary conditions (ABCs) that emulate the unbounded exterior region by absorbing outgoing electromagnetic waves. Among various ABCs, the perfectly matched layer (PML) is widely recognized for its robustness, though it typically requires multiple layers, consuming large computational resources. Recent advancements have integrated artificial neural networks (ANNs) with PML, reducing its thickness to a single layer or boundary line, thereby saving memory and computation time."
